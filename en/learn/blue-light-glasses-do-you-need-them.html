Short answer

What blue-light lenses actually do

A blue-light lens filters part of the high-energy blue band in visible light. Screens and LED lighting give off some of it, but the sun gives off far more. The lens is sold with an anti-reflective coating, and that coating is what most wearers notice: fewer reflections on the lens and less glare from a bright monitor or phone.

It is a different job from UV protection. UV is invisible and comes from the sun; blue light is part of the light you see. If you want the side-by-side, read blue-light filtering vs UV protection. For how blue-light filtering sits next to anti-reflective and UV coatings, see what each lens coating does.

What the evidence says

The American Academy of Ophthalmology explains that tired eyes at a screen come mostly from blinking less while concentrating, and it has found no scientific evidence that blue light from screens damages the eyes. A 2023 Cochrane systematic review found no evidence that blue-light filtering lenses reduce eye strain from screens, and no clear effect on sleep or on the health of the retina.

Buy blue-light lenses for glare comfort if you want it, not as protection for your eyes.

What helps screen eye strain more reliably: taking regular breaks and wearing an up-to-date prescription. An out-of-date prescription makes you work harder at every distance, with or without a blue-light filter.

Who they make sense for

Who it may suit

  • People at a computer most of the working day, including remote workers and digital nomads
  • Anyone bothered by glare or reflections on their lenses under office lighting
  • People already buying new lenses, where the price step is small

Who may not need it

  • People who rarely use screens
  • Anyone hoping it will fix tired eyes, headaches or sleep
  • Anyone whose main need is sun protection outdoors

If you spend long days on screens and also want a lens designed around screen distances, Essilor Eyezen is a separate option; see Essilor Eyezen for heavy screen use.

Children and screens

There is no good evidence that blue-light lenses protect children's eyes or reduce strain either; the Cochrane review above covered adults. What matters more is limiting screen time, regular breaks and a proper eye test if a child squints, rubs their eyes or holds a tablet very close. If a child already wears glasses and a parent wants the glare benefit, a blue-light lens can be added to the prescription. For children who are becoming short-sighted, read choosing glasses for nearsighted children.

FAQ

Do blue-light glasses reduce eye strain?

The evidence says no. A 2023 Cochrane review found no evidence that blue-light filtering lenses reduce eye strain from screens. Breaks and an up-to-date prescription help more.

How much are blue-light lenses at Manh Long Optical?

Can I get blue-light lenses without a prescription?

Yes. Non-prescription blue-light lenses are priced as the 1.56 index of each lens line.

Can I wear blue-light glasses all day?

Yes. Wearing them all day does no harm; they are simply optional.

Can you put blue-light lenses in my own frame?

Yes. Fitting new lenses into a frame you bring is free; you pay for the lenses. Staff check the frame first.

Do blue-light lenses change my prescription?

No. The filter is a lens feature, not a treatment, and it has no effect on short-sightedness.

One thing to remember

A blue-light lens is a comfort choice for glare, not eye protection; an up-to-date prescription matters more.

A note on health: This article is general information, not medical advice. If you have sudden changes in vision, eye pain or persistent headaches, see an eye doctor.
