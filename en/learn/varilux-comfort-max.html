Varilux Comfort Max: All-Day Comfort Progressives

Written by Alvin · Manh Long Optical, 147A Le Duan, Cua Nam, Hanoi · Updated 11 September 2026

In short: Varilux Comfort Max is Essilor's progressive lens built for comfort rather than sharpness. The main point isn't clarity, it's posture: a wider intermediate and near zone so you're not tilting your head to find the sharp spot. Particularly worthwhile for people over forty who sit at a desk all day and get a stiff neck or shoulders. If you need the widest possible distance zone, Physio Extensee is the better spend.

What customers actually complain about

The most common complaint from progressive-lens wearers here isn't blurry vision. It's the neck. People tilt their head slightly to find the reading zone, hold that position for an hour, and end up with sore shoulders by the afternoon, then come in to swap frames.

Varilux Comfort Max was built to fix exactly that. Not a sharper lens, an easier one.

What Comfort Max is

It's the premium progressive lens in the Varilux family, an upgrade from the older Varilux Comfort. One lens carries distance, intermediate and near power with no visible line. The surface is computed by software for each individual prescription, not moulded from a fixed template.

The manufacturer targets people in their forties and fifties who sit at a screen and switch tasks constantly. Not athletes. Not people who only need distance vision. Desk workers.

Flex Optim, the part worth explaining

Flex Optim is Essilor's method for widening the clear-vision zones, especially intermediate and near. Older progressives give you a corridor: a narrow lane of sharpness that your eyes have to line up with exactly. Comfort Max widens that lane.

In practice, what you actually feel is fairly small: less head-turning. Reading a document on the desk, glancing up to see who walked in, looking back down, the zone shift happens without hunting for it. The difference is quiet, not dramatic. Nobody tries a pair and gasps.

The "22 postures" claim, and how much to trust it

Essilor states the design supports up to 22 comfortable viewing postures. That's the manufacturer's number, from the manufacturer's lab. There's no way to re-measure that in a shop on Le Duan, and we won't pretend we can.

What we can report is what returning customers actually say. Those who notice a difference talk about the neck and shoulders, not sharper vision. Whether it's 22 postures or seven, we can't say.

Swim and adaptation

Swim is the swaying sensation at the edge of a progressive lens when you walk or turn your head. It's what makes people give up on progressives in the first week. The digital freeform surface reduces this considerably. Not to zero. Reduces it.

Here's the honest part, and the part shops often skip. A mid-range lens fitted correctly always beats a premium lens fitted wrong. If your last pair made you dizzy, that's usually a fitting error, not a lens fault, a two-millimetre error in optical centre height puts the reading zone somewhere your eyes have to reach down for on their own.

Coatings and UV

Paired with Crizal Rock or Crizal Sapphire HR. Rock for scratch resistance, Sapphire HR if you want stronger anti-reflective performance. Both resist water and dust, and cut down headlight halos, something progressive wearers over fifty bring up when talking about night driving.

UV400 protection is built into the lens material itself, along with some blue-violet light filtering. It's included, not an add-on.

Comfort Max suits you if

  • You sit at a desk most of the day and end it with a stiff neck or sore shoulders.
  • You switch constantly between a screen, papers on your desk and your phone, dozens of times an hour.
  • This is your first time moving to progressives and you want the easiest one to adapt to.
  • You gave up on an older progressive because the clear zone felt too narrow to wear comfortably.

You don't need it yet if

  • Your main need is distance vision with very little close reading, that money is better spent on a thinner index and a better coating.
  • You want the widest possible distance zone. That's Physio Extensee's territory.
  • You're set on reusing an old frame and it's a flat, shallow shape. There may not be enough depth for the near-vision zone. Bring the frame in first.

Fitting at Manh Long Optical

A progressive lens is only as good as three numbers under it: the prescription, pupillary distance and optical centre height. We start with an auto-refractor, then confirm with an actual trial-frame test, never cutting straight from a machine reading. Pupillary distance and centre height are measured on the exact frame you've chosen, worn the way you actually wear glasses.

Testing, cutting and fine-tuning are done in a single visit at 147A Le Duan, Cua Nam. If we think Comfort Max isn't the right lens for how you work, we'll say so before you pay.

The first week

Wear them all day, every day, from day one. Switching back and forth with your old glasses roughly doubles the adjustment time, and some people never adapt that way. Move your eyes to find the zone, not your head. After a few days it stops being something you think about.

Take stairs slowly for the first two or three days. That's the one place a new progressive can genuinely catch someone out.

Common questions

How much does Varilux Comfort Max cost?
Price depends on index and any coatings you add, so we quote after the eye test, not before. Call or message Zalo 0777 986 999 for a price against your exact prescription.
Should I choose Comfort Max or Physio Extensee?
Comfort Max if neck and shoulder strain from desk work is your complaint. Physio Extensee if you feel the clear zone is too narrow, especially looking into the distance. Both are premium, solving two different problems.
I've never worn progressives, can I adapt?
Most first-time wearers adjust within a few days to two weeks. What decides it is the fitting, not the lens tier, pupillary distance and optical centre height measured on the exact frame you choose.
Can Comfort Max go into a rimless or half-rim frame?
Yes, with the right index and enough frame depth. A frame that's too flat cuts into the near-vision zone. Bring the frame in and we'll tell you before you order.
Is there a warranty?
Yes, genuine Essilor warranty. Lenses arrive still sealed with a warranty card, and Manh Long Optical processes it on the spot at 147A Le Duan.
Is it worth upgrading from an ordinary progressive to Comfort Max?
Worth it if your day-to-day complaint is a narrow near zone and a stiff neck. If an ordinary progressive has never bothered you, there's nothing here you need.
Can Comfort Max be combined with a photochromic tint?
Yes. The progressive design and the photochromic layer are separate choices, so Transitions can be added on top of this lens. Ask when you order at 147A Le Duan.
Why do some people get dizzy wearing progressives?
Mostly a fitting issue, not the lens. A few millimetres off on optical centre height puts the reading zone in the wrong place, and your eyes strain all day to compensate. Upgrading the lens tier doesn't fix a bad measurement.
How long do the lenses last?
The lenses themselves, for years. What expires is your prescription. Presbyopia keeps progressing through your forties and fifties, so plan on a check every one to two years, don't wait until reading feels tiring again.
I'm only three days into new progressives and still feel dizzy, is that normal?
Normal. Mild dizziness or eye strain in the first few days is a common reaction while your eyes learn the new zones. If it's still constant after about a week, come back to 147A Le Duan so we can recheck the fitting, re-measuring and adjusting is free for life. If the dizziness hasn't eased after a week, see an eye specialist, since it may not be related to the glasses at all.
