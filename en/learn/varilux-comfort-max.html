Essilor Varilux Comfort Max: how it works and what it costs in Hanoi

Written by Alvin · Manh Long Optical, 147A Le Duan, Cua Nam, Hanoi · Updated 11 October 2026 · Part of the guide Multifocal lenses explained

In short: Varilux Comfort Max is Essilor's progressive lens that succeeded Varilux Comfort 3.0. Its Flex Optim design aims to let you see sharply from more head positions, especially at desk and screen distances, without giving up sharpness at distance or near. At Manh Long Optical a pair of lenses costs 6,780,000₫ to 30,580,000₫, frame not included, and usually takes four to five days because the lenses are surfaced at Essilor's lab. If the change between near and far bothers you more than your posture, compare it with Varilux Physio 3.0.

What Varilux Comfort Max is

Varilux Comfort Max is a progressive lens: one lens that corrects distance, intermediate and near vision, with no visible line. Essilor introduced it in 2020 as the successor to Varilux Comfort 3.0, a lens Essilor's US materials call Comfort W2+.

Each lens is calculated for your prescription rather than taken from a fixed mould. Essilor says the design starts from one of 15 vision profiles, built from data on 1,225 Varilux wearers, and then adjusts the progression to your prescription.

Essilor lists four benefits: sharp vision at all distances, a large reading area, more postural flexibility and easy adaptation. It is not a lens that trades sharpness for comfort. What Essilor claims is more positions from which you see sharply. On our price list it sits between Varilux Liberty 3.0 and Varilux Physio 3.0.

Who should consider it

Comfort Max is worth a look if:

  • You spend long stretches at a screen or desk and catch yourself tilting your head to find the clear spot.
  • You switch often between a monitor, papers and a phone.
  • You are new to progressives. In Essilor's own 2019 study of 53 first-time progressive wearers in France, 44 said they got used to the lens without thinking about it.
  • An older progressive felt too narrow at arm's length.

You may not need it if:

  • You mostly need distance vision and read very little up close. A single-vision lens or an entry progressive may be enough.
  • The change between near and far bothers you more than your posture. Compare it with Physio 3.0 below.
  • You want to reuse a very shallow frame. A progressive needs enough depth below the pupil for the reading zone, so bring the frame in and we will check it before you order.

How Flex Optim works

Every progressive lens has areas at the sides where vision blurs because of unwanted astigmatism. Where you see sharply depends on where your eyes pass through the lens and how you hold your head.

Flex Optim is Essilor's name for adjusting the progression profile to each wearer's prescription. The goal Essilor sets is postural flexibility: the range of head positions from which you can still see a target sharply. In its 2020 white paper, Essilor reports better intermediate vision than Comfort 3.0, without a significant change at distance or near.

At the counter, people who notice a difference usually describe it at the desk: less searching for the clear spot when they look from the screen to the keyboard or across the room. Some notice very little. Both are normal.

What "up to 227 more postures" means

Essilor says Comfort Max gives up to 227 more postures to see sharply. The number needs its context:

  • It comes from Essilor's 2019 computer simulations with virtual wearers, not from measuring people.
  • It counts head positions, in one-degree steps, from which the virtual wearer still sees sharply, losing no more than 0.15 logMAR of acuity (about one and a half lines on a letter chart).
  • The test case was a plano prescription with a +2.00 addition, looking at targets 65 cm and 76 cm away, which are typical computer distances.
  • The comparison is with Varilux Comfort 3.0, not with every progressive lens on the market.

So the figure is a design comparison between two Essilor lenses at screen distance. It is not a number of positions you will count yourself, and results vary with prescription, frame and fitting. Some 2020 launch coverage in the UK quoted 258; Essilor's product pages use 227, and so do we.

The 46% useful vision zone figure

Essilor also states that the useful vision zone is up to 46% larger than with Comfort 3.0. Its footnote describes this as an average gain in area across three prescriptions (−4.00, 0.00 and +4.00, each with a +2.00 addition) and five distances from 40 cm to 5 m. It is calculated, not measured on wearers, and it is not a promise of 46% more for every person.

Index, coatings and options

A Comfort Max lens is three separate choices: the design, the material (index) and the coating.

  • Index. Our list has 1.50, 1.56, 1.59, 1.60, 1.67 and 1.74. A higher index makes a strong prescription thinner; it does not make the lens see better. 1.74 comes with the Crizal Rock coating only.
  • Coating. MaxAz, or Crizal Rock, which Essilor describes as its most scratch-resistant Crizal coating, with a water- and smudge-repellent top layer. Other Crizal coatings, such as Sapphire or Prevencia, are ordered from an overseas lab and quoted separately.
  • Clear or BlueUV Capture. BlueUV Capture is a lens material that filters part of the blue-violet light; Essilor states at least 20%, depending on the material. It is a separate line on the price list, not something every clear lens includes.
  • Transitions Gen S. The photochromic version, listed for indices 1.50, 1.59, 1.60, 1.67 and 1.74. It is a separate choice from the progressive design.
  • UV protection. It depends on the material and coating you choose, so ask us to confirm it for your exact configuration.
  • Fit. A Comfort Max Fit version uses additional personalised fitting measurements. On our list it costs 2,000,000₫ to 2,100,000₫ more per pair.

How long it takes, and if you are leaving Hanoi

Comfort Max is not made in the shop. The steps are:

  1. A free eye test, about 20 to 30 minutes, whether you buy or not.
  2. You choose a frame, and we measure your pupillary distance and fitting height on that frame.
  3. We order the lenses from Essilor's lab, where they are surfaced to your measurements. This usually takes four to five days, and we confirm the expected date when we place the order.
  4. You come back to collect them, and we adjust the frame to your face.

Very strong prescriptions or strong astigmatism may need custom-surfaced lenses, which can take 10 to 15 days. If you are visiting Hanoi, tell us your departure date at the eye test. If the glasses cannot be ready in time, we can ship them abroad, with the destination, cost and delivery time agreed before you order. Delivery within inner Hanoi, hotels included, is free. The measurements that decide how the lens works

A progressive lens works only as well as the numbers behind it:

  • Prescription. We start with an auto-refractor, then confirm with a trial-frame test, rather than ordering from a machine reading alone.
  • Pupillary distance and fitting height, measured on the frame you have chosen, worn the way you actually wear glasses, so the clear corridor lines up with your pupils.
  • Frame position. The frame's tilt, its distance from the eye and its wrap also change how a progressive performs, more so with stronger prescriptions. We adjust the frame to your face when you collect the glasses.

Small errors have large effects. An article in the optical trade journal Optician calculates that a 2 mm horizontal centring error with a +1.50 addition can reduce the width of clear binocular vision by about 40%. A better lens design does not make up for a wrong measurement.

Your first two weeks

  • Wear the new glasses full-time. Switching back and forth with an old pair usually makes adapting harder.
  • To look to the side, turn your head toward what you want to see. The edges of a progressive lens are not designed for sharp vision.
  • To read, lower your eyes rather than your head. For a screen, find the height where it is sharp; lowering the screen slightly often helps.
  • Take care on stairs, steps and kerbs at first. Optometry falls guidelines note a higher risk of missing a step with bifocal and progressive lenses. Tip your chin down slightly so you see the steps through the upper part of the lens.
  • Most people adjust within a few days to two weeks. Some take longer.

If something still feels wrong, come back. A free eye test and prescription re-check are included in your first two weeks, and the cause can be the prescription, a measurement, the frame position or the lens design, not only adaptation.

See an eye doctor right away for eye pain, sudden loss or blurring of vision, double vision, new flashes or floaters, or a shadow across part of your vision, even if it passes. These are not adaptation symptoms.

Is Comfort Max better than Physio 3.0?
Neither is better for everyone. Essilor builds Comfort Max around postural flexibility and Physio 3.0 around smoother transitions between distances. We usually suggest Comfort Max when desk posture is the complaint and Physio 3.0 when moving between near and far is the problem, and we decide after measuring your eyes.
Is Comfort Max good for computer work?
It is an all-distance progressive, not a dedicated office lens. Essilor's 227-postures figure was calculated at 65 cm and 76 cm, typical computer distances, compared with Varilux Comfort 3.0. If almost all of your day is at one screen distance, tell us, because a different lens type may suit you better.
Which index should I choose?
It depends on how strong your prescription is and on the frame. A higher index makes a strong prescription thinner, but it does not make the lens see better. We recommend an index after measuring your eyes and seeing the frame you choose.
Can I wear Comfort Max with a strong prescription or astigmatism?
Many people can. Whether your exact prescription can be made is confirmed when we place the order. Very strong prescriptions or strong astigmatism may need custom-surfaced lenses, which can take 10 to 15 days.
Can Comfort Max be photochromic?
