Eye-testing expertise

A trustworthy optical shop starts with a proper eye test, not just an autorefractor reading followed by an immediate prescription. A reliable process should include a trial-lens check to confirm the autorefractor's numbers, a check of each eye's vision separately, astigmatism measurement, and pupillary distance (PD) — not stop at a single machine reading.

At Manh Long Optical, a full eye test runs through all four of those steps, takes about 20-30 minutes, and is free even if you're not sure you'll buy. Re-checks afterward are also free with no limit on how many times, so there's no need to hesitate about getting tested without committing to a purchase.

Product range

A wide product range doesn't mean a lot of random styles — it means enough choice for specific needs: multiple frame materials for different budgets and durability levels, multiple lens indexes for different prescription strengths, and functional lens types (photochromic, blue-light-filtering, progressive) for different lifestyles.

Manh Long Optical currently stocks around 1,000 frame styles, across four materials — titanium, acetate, flexible plastic and metal — priced 550,000 to 3,500,000 VND. Common questions

What should I ask before an eye test at an optical shop?
Ask clearly how many steps the test includes, whether it uses a trial-lens check to confirm the autorefractor reading or relies on the machine alone, and whether there's a fee for the test.
Should I worry about a cheap price on glasses?
Not necessarily, but ask what's included in that price (frame, lens, coatings) so you're comparing like for like — many shops quote the frame and lens separately, and the real total only becomes clear once you add both.
Are Google Maps reviews fully trustworthy?
It helps to also check the number of reviews and read a few recent ones, not just the average star rating, since a high score with very few reviews doesn't always reflect the broader customer experience.
What if a shop doesn't offer a frame warranty?
Worth thinking carefully about, since a manufacturing defect in a frame is a risk the customer has no control over; a trustworthy shop usually has a clear exchange policy for exactly that case.
