Lens index: 1.56, 1.60, 1.67 or 1.74 for your prescription

Manh Long Optical · 147A Le Duan, Cua Nam, Hanoi · Updated 10 September 2026

In short: The higher the lens index, the thinner the lens at the same prescription, and the higher the price. At Manh Long Optical the usual advice is 1.60 for short-sightedness under 3 dioptres, 1.67 for 3 to 5, and 1.74 above 5. Choosing 1.74 for a mild prescription means paying a lot more for thinness you will barely see.

Same lens line, same shop: Essilor Blue UV Capture costs 1,298,000₫ a pair at index 1.56 and 12,380,000₫ at 1.74. That gap is why it pays to know which index you need before you choose. Index is labelled the same way everywhere, so a 1.67 here is a 1.67 at home, and you can hold these figures against any quote you already have.

1. What lens index means

Index is a number for how strongly the lens material bends light. The higher it is, the thinner and lighter the lens at the same prescription, and the difference grows as the prescription gets stronger.

Index does not change how sharply you see. That comes from an accurate prescription; index only decides how thick and heavy the lens sits in the frame. At mild prescriptions the steps are hard to tell apart. At strong ones you see them at the lens edge.

It is a starting point. A small frame hides thickness better than a large one, so a strong prescription can sometimes sit neatly on a lower index. The guidance is for short-sightedness only; long-sight and strong astigmatism need the eye test first.

A higher index does not automatically mean a wider prescription range. It depends on the line. Between -10.00 and -12.00, Elements makes a 1.67 but not a 1.74.

Astigmatism is the other difference. In the Essilor and ZEISS lines only the 1.74 takes astigmatism up to 6.00; 1.56 to 1.67 take up to 2.00, and Elements takes up to 2.00 at all four indices. For astigmatism above 2.00 our list has a separate high-astigmatism group, quoted after the eye test.

4. The big jump is at 1.74

In the Essilor line, going from 1.67 to 1.74 adds 8,400,000₫, more than the two earlier steps combined (2,682,000₫). ZEISS follows the same pattern. Its ClearView 1.67 to 1.74 step adds 8,080,000₫.

For that money, the 1.74 lenses from both brands go to -18.00 and -20.00 with astigmatism up to 6.00, where the 1.67 stops at -10.00 and 2.00. If you sit near the 5-dioptre line, have the eye test first, then ask how thick the edge of a 1.67 and a 1.74 would be in the frame you have chosen.

5. When a high index is not worth it

Mild prescriptions rarely need it. Under 3 dioptres we suggest 1.60, and at that level the thickness difference between indices is hard to see, while the price difference is not: in Essilor Blue UV Capture, 1.60 is 2,280,000₫ and 1.74 is 12,380,000₫.

On a tight budget, look at Elements. It lets you move up an index for a smaller step in price, as long as your prescription falls inside what that line makes. And if you are above 5 dioptres, saving money with a low index usually means a thick edge and a heavier pair you wear all day.

Common questions

What is the difference between 1.60 and 1.67 lenses?
A 1.67 lens is thinner than a 1.60 at the same prescription, and in the Essilor and ZEISS lines it goes to -10.00 instead of -8.00. Our usual advice is 1.60 under 3 dioptres and 1.67 from 3 to 5; in Essilor Blue UV Capture, 1.67 costs 1,700,000₫ more than 1.60.
Do I need 1.74 lenses for a -2.00 prescription?
Usually not. Under 3 dioptres we suggest 1.60; at mild prescriptions the difference in thickness between indices is hard to see, while in Essilor Blue UV Capture 1.60 is 2,280,000₫ and 1.74 is 12,380,000₫.
Does a higher index always cover a stronger prescription?
Not always; it depends on the lens line. Elements Blue UV Cut 1.74 is made to -10.00, while Elements 1.67 goes to -12.00; in the Essilor and ZEISS lines, the higher index does cover the stronger prescription.
