Start with what you need

Distance only, or reading only

Single-vision lenses carry one prescription across the whole lens. This is the quickest and least expensive route, and stocked lenses are usually cut the same day.

Distance and reading in one pair

Progressive lenses combine several viewing distances with no visible line. The fitting measurements have to be exact and most people need a few days to adjust.

Lenses that darken outdoors

Photochromic lenses darken in UV light outside and clear again indoors. One pair covers the whole day.

Prescription sunglasses

Tinted prescription lenses stay dark and do not fade indoors. Better suited to long hours outside or daytime driving.

A strong prescription

Higher-index lenses are thinner at the same prescription. Frame shape also changes how much thickness you actually see at the edge.

Keeping your own frame

New lenses in an existing frame is usually possible. The frame is checked first, and you are told if it has gone brittle and may crack during re-fitting.

Which index to choose

Index is the number that tells you how thin a lens is at a given prescription. Higher means thinner and lighter, and the difference becomes easier to see as the prescription gets stronger. The usual guidance in store:

Your prescriptionIndex usually chosen
Under 3 dioptres1.60
3 to 5 dioptres1.67
Above 5, or a rimless frame1.74

That is a starting point, not a rule. A smaller frame hides thickness better than a large one, so a strong prescription in a small frame can sit fine on a lower index.

Coatings: which earn their money

Anti-reflective is the one worth having. It cuts reflections off the lens surface, so screens and night driving are less glaring, and people looking at you see your eyes instead of two bright patches.

Scratch and water resistance extends the life of the lens, which matters if you clean your glasses on your shirt.

Blue-light filtering deserves a straight answer. A 2023 systematic review from Cochrane found no evidence that this filter reduces eye strain, and the American Academy of Ophthalmology puts screen-related strain down mainly to blinking less while concentrating. The real benefit left is the glare reduction from the anti-reflective coating that comes with it. It is not a medical measure and does not prevent eye disease.

Common questions

Progressive lenses, or two separate pairs?
Progressives put distance and near in one pair with no visible line, but they need exact fitting and a few days to get used to. Two pairs cost less and need no adjustment, but you swap glasses every time the distance changes. People who move around and work at several distances usually get on better with progressives.
Can photochromic lenses replace sunglasses?
For most of daily life, yes. But they darken in response to UV, and a car windscreen filters much of the UV out, so inside a car they often stay lighter than you expect. Anyone who drives a lot in daylight is still better off with a separate prescription tint.
Do glasses reduce short-sightedness?
No. Glasses are a vision aid: they let you see clearly while you wear them, and they do not change the prescription itself. Anyone telling you otherwise is worth questioning.
How often should I have my eyes tested?
Adults, usually every 6 to 12 months, sooner if things look blurrier, if you find yourself squinting, or if reading gives you a headache. At Manh Long Optical re-testing is free for life, including when you buy nothing.
Should I bring my old prescription?
Bring it if you have it, it is a good starting point. If not, no problem, the eye test is free and the measurements are taken from scratch. A prescription older than 12 months is re-tested rather than copied, because eyes change.
Can I pay by card?
Yes. Cash, credit and debit cards are all accepted.
