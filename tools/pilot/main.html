Photochromic vs Polarized Lenses: Which Should You Choose?

Both help in bright sun, but they solve different problems. This comparison explains what each lens actually does, which one works with a prescription, and how to decide based on how you spend your day.

By Alvin Dao, Store ManagerPublished 11 Sep 2026Updated 23 Sep 20267 min read

Short answer

Choose photochromic if you want one pair of prescription glasses that works indoors and darkens outside. Choose polarized if your main problem is glare off roads, water or car bonnets. Why people confuse these two lenses

Both lenses are sold as "sun" lenses, so it is easy to assume one is simply a better version of the other. In fact they work in completely different ways.

A polarized lens carries a fixed filter that blocks light reflected horizontally, the flat, blinding glare that bounces off tarmac, water or a car bonnet. It is always dark. A photochromic lens has no glare filter; instead it reacts to UV, darkening outdoors and clearing again in shade or indoors.

That difference matters more than any brand name: one lens is built to remove a specific kind of glare, the other is built to let a single pair of glasses do two jobs.

What to compare

  1. Prescription. Can the lens be made to your prescription, or will you need contacts underneath?
  2. Indoor wear. Can you keep the glasses on when you walk inside?
  3. Glare off roads and water. Does the lens remove reflected glare, or only reduce overall brightness?
  4. Everyday use. Could this be your only pair of glasses?
  5. Price. What does each cost at the shop, and what drives the difference?

1.Polarized lenses

Polarized lenses use a vertical filter to block horizontally reflected glare. Riders and drivers out in strong midday sun, and people who go to the beach often, usually notice the difference clearly: the road surface and the water look less blown out.

Main strengths

  • Removes glare off wet roads, water and car bonnets
  • Fixed tint that doesn't depend on UV level
  • Priced like standard sunglasses

Limitations

  • Non-prescription only at Manh Long Optical
  • Too dark to wear indoors
  • Short-sighted wearers need contact lenses underneath

Best forPeople who drive or ride a lot in daytime sun, or spend time near water.

Consider instead ifYou need your prescription in the lens and don't wear contacts. Photochromic or prescription-tinted sunglasses (page in Vietnamese) fit that need.

2.Photochromic lenses

Photochromic lenses darken automatically in sunlight through a reaction to UV, then clear again in shade or indoors. Because they can carry a prescription, one pair works as everyday glasses and doubles as sunglasses outdoors. Photochromic is also available in progressive lenses for people who need both near and distance vision.

Main strengths

  • Can be made to your prescription
  • Wearable all day, indoors and outdoors
  • Wide price range, from 350,000đ

Limitations

  • No dedicated filter for glare off road or water
  • Darkness depends on UV strength at the time
  • Takes a short while to clear after you come indoors

Best forAnyone who wears prescription glasses and moves between indoors and outdoors during the day.

Consider instead ifGlare off the road or water is what bothers you most. Polarized sunglasses handle that better.

Which should you choose?

  • If you ride a motorbike or drive in the midday sun a lot→Polarized sunglasses
  • If you go to the beach or spend time near water often→Polarized sunglasses
  • If you need one prescription pair for all day, in and out of the sun→Photochromic
  • If you're short-sighted and don't want to wear contacts→Photochromic
  • If you do both: daily prescription wear plus regular driving or beach trips→Photochromic glasses and a separate polarized pair

FAQ

Does photochromic get as dark as real sunglasses in strong sun?

Close to it, but how dark it gets depends on UV intensity at the time, so it darkens more at midday than in the late afternoon or on an overcast day.

Does the shop fit polarized with a prescription?

Not currently. Polarized lenses at Manh Long Optical are non-prescription sunglasses only.

Is there a delay when photochromic lenses go from outdoors back indoors?

Yes, anywhere from tens of seconds to a minute or two depending on brand and outdoor temperature. They don't clear instantly.

What's the lowest-priced photochromic option at the shop?

Blick at index 1.56 for 350,000đ, suited to a tight budget while still trying photochromic.

Can photochromic replace sunglasses for a beach trip or a long drive?

It reduces general brightness, but for glare off water or road surfaces, polarized still handles it better through its dedicated vertical filter, which photochromic doesn't have.

One thing to remember

Pick by the problem you want solved: polarized removes reflected glare, photochromic lets one prescription pair work indoors and out.

A note on health: Information here is for reference; prices and policy can change by brand and over time. Prescription glasses correct vision; they don't reduce a prescription or cure short-sightedness.
